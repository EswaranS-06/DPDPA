> **Drafting note:** This is the master public notice. Under Rule 3(a), the notice shown at the point of consent must be understandable on its own, without referring to other information. So use this master notice for transparency and for legitimate-use processing, and pair it with the short purpose-wise consent notices in C01. Never rely on a link to this page as the only consent notice. Write in plain language: short sentences, the second person ("you"), no unexplained legal jargon. References modelled on DPDP-aligned addenda already published by Indian entities (e.g. a BPO's "India Addendum") show the trend: a separate India section with Grievance Officer contact, the four DPDP rights, a Board-escalation path and response timelines.

## 7. How long we keep your personal data

We keep personal data only as long as it is needed for the specified purpose, or as long as a law requires us to keep it. After that we erase it, and we ask our Data Processors to erase it too (section 8(7) of the Act). Our retention periods are listed in Section 5 and in our Data Retention Schedule. In summary:
- **When you withdraw consent**, we stop processing for that purpose within [Withdrawal-Effect-Period]. We then erase the related data, unless a law requires us to keep it.
- **For security and audit**, we keep the logs of processing and the related personal data for at least **one year** from the date of processing. This lets us detect, investigate and fix unauthorised access (Rules 6(1)(e) and 8(3)).
- **For statutory records** (tax, accounting, sector regulation), we keep data for the periods those laws require, for example [Statutory-Retention-Examples].

- **Inactive accounts.** If you have not used your account, or exercised your rights, for **three years**, we will erase your personal data, except data needed for you to access your account or any stored-value / virtual token issued by us. We will tell you at least **48 hours** before erasure, and logging in or contacting us before then will stop it (Rule 8 and Third Schedule).

## 10. Your rights

|---|---|---|
| **Access information** | Get a summary of the personal data we process about you and the processing activities, the identities of all other Data Fiduciaries and Data Processors we have shared it with (with a description of what was shared), and any other information the law prescribes. | s.11 |

| **Grievance redressal** | Have a readily available way to raise a complaint about how we handle your data, and get a response within the published timeline. | s.13 |

The rights to access, correction and erasure apply to processing based on your consent or on data you voluntarily provided (section 7(a)). The right to access does not cover sharing with an authority that is legally authorised to receive data for preventing, detecting or investigating offences or cyber incidents (section 11(2)).

## 15. How we protect your personal data

We maintain reasonable security safeguards, in line with section 8(5) of the Act and Rule 6. They include:
- encryption of personal data in transit and at rest, and masking, tokenisation or obfuscation where appropriate;
- role-based access controls, multi-factor authentication and least-privilege access;
- logging and monitoring of access to personal data, with logs kept for at least one year;
- backups and business-continuity arrangements;
- security obligations in all our contracts with Data Processors;
- periodic security assessments, staff training and an incident-response plan<!-- IF Security-Certifications --> (certified to [Security-Certifications])<!-- END IF -->.

If a personal data breach affects you, we will tell you without delay. We will explain what happened, the likely consequences, what we are doing about it, the steps you can take to protect yourself, and whom to contact. We will also report it to the Data Protection Board (Rule 7).
